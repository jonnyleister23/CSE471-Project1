Effects
& mixing.

A parallel processing stage lets every note choose how much sound goes to the dry output and four distinct audio effects.

Four effects

01 / Chorus

Reads from a short delay line while slowly changing the read delay. The modulated delayed copy adds movement and thickness to the source.

02 / Flanging

Uses a much shorter moving delay with feedback, producing frequency cancellation and reinforcement that sweeps over time.

03 / Reverberation

Four feedback delays of different lengths simulate multiple reflections and create a decaying reverberant tail rather than a single repeat.

04 / Ring modulation

Multiplies the incoming sound by a 110 Hz sine carrier. This creates new sum and difference frequencies and a more metallic timbre.

Controllable effects sends

Each CInstrument stores five send levels: one dry channel and one for each effect. For every generated sample frame, the sequencer multiplies the voice's left and right output by its send levels and sums the contributions for each bus. Each persistent effect processor receives its corresponding bus; the result is mixed with the dry signal.

<note measure="1" beat="1" note="C3" duration="1.85"
      wave="saw" dry="0.76" chorus="0.08"
      flange="0.12" reverb="0.21" ring="0.04" />

A value of zero disables a particular route for that note. The XML score therefore determines the effects used during the composition. Effects are kept running across notes so delayed audio can continue after an individual voice stops.

Frame-by-frame architecture

Signal path

Active instrument voices → five stereo send buses → four persistent effects → dry + wet mix → stereo WAV output

The delay buffers only store short histories needed for the effects. They do not store the whole composition. The effect processor is defined in CEffects.h; voice sends are in CInstrument.h, and routing is handled by CSynthesizer.cpp.
